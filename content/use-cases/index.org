Manufacturing & process industry

* Your maintenance intervals come from the calendar, not from the equipment.

You direct a plant where the line is instrumented end to end. Every piece of equipment carries its own measurement: a vessel measured for pressure and oxidation, an exhauster downstream measured for temperature, and so on along the train. Those instruments report continuously and your maintenance system holds years of work orders written against the same equipment, and still the interval on which any asset is serviced comes from the calendar, the manufacturer's manual or the last shutdown. Reliability engineering is done by hand, one asset at a time, so long-lead spares arrive late and planned work keeps arriving as breakdown.

#+begin_fix
*What we do*

Source Connectors replicate the equipment master and failure history from SAP, the process signals from your historian and the alarm record from SCADA. Asset Extraction proposes the asset tree, and the Model Context Layer attaches every instrument to the equipment and variable it measures. [[/product/reliability-block-diagrams/][Reliability Block Diagrams]] record what runs in series and what is redundant.

Life Data Analysis and per-asset models then estimate each asset's probability of failure, and System Reliability and Failure Propagation state what that means for the line. The result is a maintenance window for each asset, with the probability behind the date and the records it was built from. Your engineers stop assembling the analysis and start deciding on it.
#+end_fix

#+begin_faq
* Common questions

** What does a plant send us?

Read access to the systems that already hold its history: the equipment master and maintenance history, usually in SAP; the process historian; SCADA or alarm logs; and any existing reliability studies. We replicate the data and do not change the source systems.

** How much failure history is needed?

Fewer failures than people assume, because units that have not failed carry information too. Where the record will not support a useful estimate, the asset is reported as one we cannot yet speak about rather than given a number.

** Where does symbolic AI come in?

In the description of the plant. The asset tree, the component catalogue, the variable mappings and the block diagrams are explicit structure derived by rules, which the statistics and machine-learning models are then fitted over. Each prediction can therefore name the asset, the variables and the dependencies it rests on.

** Is Contour only for manufacturing?

No. Reliability and maintenance is where it is most developed. The same platform serves funds, fintechs and hospitals, wherever the records exist and their meaning has never been written down.

** How does an engagement start?

With your source systems rather than with a demonstration. The model of the plant is built first and validated against records you already trust, before any estimate is placed on top of it.
